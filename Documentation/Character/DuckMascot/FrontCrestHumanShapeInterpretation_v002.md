## Core complete-form definition

Crest L, Crest C and Crest R are three independent **soft elongated oval-like forms**.

Their shared construction language is:

- one continuous rounded volume;
- broad sides;
- rounded upper end;
- rounded concealed lower end;
- restrained curvature;
- no sharp root;
- no narrow neck;
- no inward lower-middle pinch;
- no waist caused by a neighboring Crest element.

They are not perfect geometric ellipses, but they read substantially closer to softly bent elongated ovals than to beans, hooks or teardrops.

The following interpretations are explicitly rejected:

- hook shapes;
- bean shapes;
- teardrop roots;
- narrow-waisted leaf shapes;
- curved stems;
- tabs or crescents;
- platform-connected shapes.

## Crest L

Crest L is interpreted as a short-to-medium elongated soft oval.

It:

- leans slightly left;
- permits only mild curvature;
- remains broad through the middle and lower body;
- has rounded upper and concealed lower ends;
- has no concave inner waist;
- has no right-facing hook.

Its complete underlying oval remains intact where its Front projection intersects C.

## Crest C

Crest C is interpreted as the tallest and visually dominant elongated soft oval.

It:

- is mostly upright with a natural slight lean;
- remains broad on both sides;
- is deliberately wider through the middle and lower-middle than v001;
- may have only a very mild waist, if any;
- has rounded upper and concealed lower ends;
- has no pinched neck;
- has no droplet-shaped lower bulb;
- does not become a platform for L or R.

Its complete form remains continuous where L and R intersect it in projection.

## Crest R

Crest R is interpreted as the shortest elongated soft oval.

It:

- leans right;
- permits only mild curvature;
- remains broad and full;
- has rounded upper and concealed lower ends;
- is not a crescent, hook or tab.

Its intersection with C does not deform R's complete underlying oval.

## Spatial relationship

All three complete forms:

- belong to the same Local Plane;
- grow toward the same Head attachment surface;
- remain independent complete elements;
- may intersect in the Front projection;
- have no permanent Z-depth priority.

Projected intersections are visual consequences of projection. They do not cut, pinch or reshape the complete underlying forms.

Specifically:

- C does not create a concave waist in L;
- L and R do not pinch C's sides;
- C does not turn R into a hook;
- no element becomes another element's base or continuation.
